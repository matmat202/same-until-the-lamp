Same Until the Lamp · a story

Every pair comes apart the same way

Same Until the Lamp shows two squares a day that match in daylight and stop matching under a warm bulb. Here are all days at once, each pair drawn as one dot in the colour it shows in daylight, on a map of colour: green to the left, red to the right, yellow up, blue down.

The pairs come in every colour there is.

They sit in a ring because the program aims each day at a random hue of the same strength.

Chapter 1 · The bulb goes on

All the lines are parallel

Switch on the bulb and let your eyes adjust, and each dot splits in two. A line joins one square’s new colour to the other’s.

Teal, orange, pink, olive: whatever colour the pair started as, its line points the same way, from green over to magenta.

Chapter 2 · One direction

A fan wide

Move every line so it starts at the centre and they make one narrow fan: all directions lie within of each other, out of a possible 180.

The squares hardly change in lightness. Typically they end up units apart in colour but only in lightness.

And it is always the same square that goes magenta: on all days, the one that reflects more light above 620 nm, the red end of the rainbow.

The bulb gives off more red light and less blue than daylight. The two paints balance out under daylight, so the bulb tips that balance towards whichever paint leans on the red end.

Chapter 3 · Not the optimiser

Pairs nobody chose lean the same way

The page’s pairs are not random: a program picked each one to come apart as far as possible. Perhaps the fan is the program’s habit. So here are metameric pairs nobody optimised: a random paint, plus and minus a random curve that daylight cannot see.

Chapter 4 · Where the paints differ

Every pair’s curves cross at least three times

Each square is a curve: how much light the paint reflects at each wavelength. For two different curves to look the same to an eye with three kinds of cone, the textbooks say, they must cross at least three times. They do: pairs cross three times, cross four times, and none fewer.

The crossings fall in three places: around (blue), (green) and (orange). Past the last one, one paint reflects more red light than the other, and the bulb is mostly red light.

Epilogue · Today’s pair

Try it on today’s pair

Same Until the Lamp has a new pair every day. Switch on the bulb and watch which square goes magenta.

The colours are the page’s own: reflectance curves built from smooth bumps, CIE 1931 2° observer from CVRL, daylight is CIE illuminant D65, the bulb is CIE illuminant A (a 2848 K tungsten lamp), and the eye’s adjustment is CAT02. Directions are on the CIELAB a*b* plane. A screen only simulates this, and a different bulb (an LED, say) would push in its own direction; this counts only the one lamp the page uses.
